# Reading guide: Transaction Fee Mechanism Design

This guide sets a reading order and points at the original text. It does not
summarise the paper: read each unit in the paper itself.

The agent read sections 2 and 3 in full, so units 1 to 10 were built from the
text. Sections 1, 4, 5 and 6 are listed from their headings and theorem names
only; their order below is provisional.

## Why the order differs from the paper

Sections 2 and 3 already define each term before using it, so they keep the
paper's order. The introduction does not: it states the main results using
MMIC, DSIC and OCA-proof, which are defined in section 3. Read it last.

## Units, in reading order

Each unit: where it is, what it needs, and a question to answer for yourself
before you read it.

1. **Blockchain transactions** — section 2.1 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S2.SS1))
   Needs: nothing.
   Before you read: a block has limited room. Who do you expect decides which transactions go into it?

2. **The basic model** — section 2.2 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S2.SS2))
   Needs: unit 1.
   Before you read: each transaction gets a size, a valuation and a bid. Which of the three can other people not see, and why would that matter?

3. **Allocation rules** — Definitions 2.1, 2.2 and Remark 2.3, section 2.3.1 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S2.SS3.SSS1))
   Needs: unit 2; mechanism design (chapter 0).
   Before you read: the protocol can say which transactions *should* be included. Can it force that?

4. **Payment and burning rules** — Definitions 2.4, 2.5 and Remarks 2.7, 2.8, section 2.3.2 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S2.SS3.SSS2))
   Needs: unit 3.
   Before you read: why might a protocol destroy part of a fee instead of giving all of it to the miner?

5. **First-price auction as an example** — Example 2.6 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S2.Thmtheorem6))
   Needs: units 3, 4.
   Before you read: write Bitcoin's "pay what you bid" rule as three separate rules: who gets in, who pays the miner what, what is burned.

6. **Transaction fee mechanism** — Definition 2.9 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S2.Thmtheorem9))
   Needs: units 3, 4.
   Before you read: nothing to predict; this names the triple from units 3 and 4.

7. **Users and DSIC** — Definition 3.1 and the paragraph after it, section 3.1 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S3.SS1))
   Needs: unit 6; dominant strategy (chapter 0).
   Before you read: in a pay-what-you-bid auction, should you bid exactly what the transaction is worth to you?

8. **Myopic miners and MMIC** — Remark 3.2, Definitions 3.3, 3.4, section 3.2 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S3.SS2))
   Needs: unit 6 (unit 5 for Remark 3.2).
   Before you read: besides choosing which transactions to include, what else could a miner do to a block to earn more?

9. **Second-price auctions are not MMIC** — Example 3.5 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S3.Thmtheorem5))
   Needs: unit 8; second-price auction (chapter 0).
   Before you read: three slots, top bids 10, 8 and 3, everyone pays the lowest winning bid. Find a way for the miner to earn more than 9.

10. **Off-chain agreements and OCA-proof** — Definitions 3.6, 3.7, section 3.3 ([link](https://arxiv.org/html/2106.01340v3#S3.SS3))
    Needs: units 7, 8; Pareto improvement (chapter 0).
    Before you read: suppose part of every fee is burned. What private deal could a miner and a user make that leaves both better off?
